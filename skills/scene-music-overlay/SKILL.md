# Scene Music Overlay

Use a supplied player screenshot as authoritative. Otherwise choose one identifiable player family and keep its album art, title/artist hierarchy, progress bar, controls, typography, and colours coherent. Never invent a generic music HUD.

## Choose music content

1. Use exact supplied song, artist, album art, player screenshot, or playlist.
2. If a mood/artist/genre is requested without a track, recommend a fitting real track only when invited, and state the selection.
3. If no music instruction is supplied, browse the official current Billboard Hot 100 and randomly select one entry. Use title and artist without claiming it is the user's real listening activity. If access is unavailable, ask the user for a song; do not invent a demo track.

## Choose one composition

- **Playlist:** oversized backgroundless playlist field across 55–75% canvas height; rows, cover tiles, separators and controls float directly on scene with deliberately high transparency. Let the list dissolve into the photo rather than reading as a solid screen.
- **One song:** default to one large, substantial frosted player card in the main background plane behind the person—wide across an open wall/facade or tall beside portrait architecture. The person should break the card's edge. Use a perspective-bound projection on a floor/table only when shoes, legs, or a first-person downward view are the composition's primary subject; a visible floor alone is not enough.
- **Multiple music elements explicitly requested:** 3–5 unequal, semi-transparent frosted player cards from one player family. One is dominant; distribute the rest around the person along a real wall, mirror, staircase, or scene axis, with varied overlap and depth.

## Integration

Place all UI behind the subject when overlapping. Preserve face, eyes, hands, phone, held objects, and clothing silhouette. Anchor to a real wall/floor/mirror plane; match perspective, light, grain and reflections. A one-song hero card should be opaque-to-semi-opaque frosted glass so metadata and controls feel intentional; playlists should be more transparent; multi-card constellations should use lighter overlapping glass. Use solid panels only when supplied source requires them.

For a supplied player screenshot, preserve its outer aspect ratio and the relative geometry of cover art, title/artist block, progress bar, time labels, controls, padding, and radius. Scale the player as one coherent system; do not turn a wide player into a tall card or independently stretch/reposition its anatomy. Perspective and natural foreground occlusion are the only allowed adaptations.

A single-player card normally occupies 35–60% of canvas area or 45–75% of usable background height. It must read as the main edit at thumbnail size, not as a peripheral badge.

Avoid equal card grids, competing music brands, waveform filler, tiny players, opaque playlist screens, floor projections when feet are not the image's primary visual, sticker shadows, generic glow, distorted source-player proportions, and album art that dominates the person.
