# Scene Activity Overlay

Make the movement photo the anchor. Treat data as printed, projected, or composed within the photograph—not pasted as a dashboard. Use exact supplied data; otherwise keep any demonstration clearly non-factual and minimal.

## Default: photo-native performance typography

- Place data directly on sky, road, pavement, grass, wall, or broad textured ground. Do not use framed dashboards, frosted cards, app chrome, or stickers by default.
- Build one mass: airy top grid, vertical stack, two-column side field, or oversized type grounded on pavement.
- Use one hero value (distance, pace, time, or ring) plus 2–6 supporting values. Labels stay small; values substantially larger.
- Let runner/shoes interrupt type naturally, but protect face, hands, movement silhouette, joints, and held objects.
- Match grain, perspective, contrast, and substrate texture; on pavement, weather type slightly rather than making it pristine.

## Adapt supplied fitness UI

Reuse actual metrics, hierarchy, accent colour, route shape, rings, and icon language, but deconstruct and recompose them directly in the photo. Show a full app screen only when explicitly requested or supported by a believable physical surface.

## Layout choices

- **Airy metric field:** up to six metrics across calm sky/road/lawn.
- **Hero stat poster:** one huge number on pavement/wall/sky with tight support labels.
- **Route editorial:** one supplied route trace plus 2–3 metrics; never outline the athlete.
- **Activity summary:** compact supplied ring/chart and a few metrics, with no container unless the scene has a real screen.

## Mandatory visual self-check

Before delivering an activity edit, inspect it at full size and thumbnail size. Revise once when any answer below is “no”:

- **Scene continuity:** Does the real surface texture, lighting, grain, and perspective continue through or around the data? A darkening or haze may support legibility, but it must not read as a detached rectangle.
- **Foreground depth:** Is the subject still the first read, with at least one natural overlap between the person/gear and the data field when the composition permits? Never force this overlap across the face, hands, or movement-critical body parts.
- **Card test:** If the data block could be cropped out as a generic floating app card, deconstruct it into type, rings, and dividers placed directly in the scene instead.
- **Data check:** Are every supplied number, unit, label, and accent element reproduced accurately, with no invented metrics or pseudo-text?
- **Thumbnail read:** Does the image read as activity/photo first, then one hero metric—not as a screenshot pasted on a photograph?

Avoid neon HUDs, generic dashboard cards, card grids, sticker borders, fake app chrome, excessive glow, inconsistent units, invented health claims, and decorative route outlines.
